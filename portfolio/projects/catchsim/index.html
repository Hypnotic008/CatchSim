CatchSim: Super Heavy return and tower catch

A 6-DOF, quaternion-based simulation of a Super Heavy V3 booster from hot-stage separation to the chopstick catch. It has closed-loop guidance for every phase, a 100-case Monte Carlo campaign and a 3-D render of the full flight.

Mission overview

Monte Carlo robustness

Each case plans on the nominal models, flies a dispersed truth plant and guides on what it can measure in flight. Dispersions cover separation state, per-engine thrust, Isp, mass, drag, air density, surface winds up to 12 m/s, jet streams up to 60 m/s and wind-forecast error.

From 1/41 to 101/101

The starting point caught the booster on the nominal trajectory, but a 2 km shift in the separation point missed the tower by about 1 km. Each fix below came from a diagnosed failure:

  1. Continuous boostback predictor. Stage switches now land exactly on step boundaries, and an Illinois-secant solver re-solves cutoff time and heading every 1.5 s during the burn.
  2. In-flight estimation. IMU-style thrust-to-mass and tank-gauge mass-flow estimators remove the dominant thrust and Isp dispersion errors.
  3. Grid-fin entry guidance. Predicts the arrival point and commands angle of attack within fin authority, using a wind forecast and an in-flight drag estimator.
  4. Unified landing law. Energy matching to a gate, then one constant-deceleration arc into the chopsticks, with a 3-D thrust-vector solve that includes estimated drag and lift.
  5. Disturbance observer. Cancels steady surface wind at the catch by comparing measured acceleration with what the actual thrust vector should produce.
  6. Attitude and roll. A controlled RCS slew to engines-first during the coast, with catch roll set in vacuum and locked through the burn.
